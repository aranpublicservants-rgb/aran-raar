From experience to action

Share a practical lesson about implementing the Clerk’s Call to Action. Describe what worked, what did not work and what others could do next.

Contributions are added to a shared working draft, organized by theme. It includes a structure for evidence review, findings and recommendations that can support a future paper.

Read the working draft
Before you contribute

The working draft is public. Use general descriptions. Do not include names, email addresses, case details or protected information. Contributions are self-reported and are not verified findings or network positions.

A withdrawal code is provided after submission. Keep it if you may want to remove your contribution.

Read the Clerk’s Call to Action

Before you send: public contributions

Anyone with the working-draft link can read a submitted lesson immediately. The volunteer coordinating team can use it in the draft and a future paper. Do not include names, contact details, identifiable cases or protected information.

There is no automatic expiry. A lesson remains in the active draft until it is withdrawn. Keep your reference and withdrawal code to remove it. Withdrawal cannot recall copies already downloaded or shared.

See three example lessons

Illustrative examples only. These fictional, anonymous examples show the structure; they are not submissions, findings or claims about an organization.

Making meetings easier to access
Context and barrier
Materials arrived too late for participants to prepare.
Action taken
The organizer shared an accessible agenda and offered written input.
What changed
Participants had another way to contribute; attendance alone could not show whether access improved.
Evidence and limits
A short feedback check would be needed; no measured result is claimed.
Lesson learned
Offer more than one way to take part.
Recommended next step
Ask the meeting coordinator to test the options and review feedback after two meetings.
Sharing access to development opportunities
Context and barrier
Development opportunities were shared through informal contacts.
Action taken
A manager tested a common notice with clear criteria and a response period.
What changed
The process became easier to explain; its effect on access remained to be assessed.
Evidence and limits
Selection and feedback data would be needed before drawing conclusions.
Lesson learned
Make the process visible before judging its results.
Recommended next step
Ask the responsible manager to review who learned about and accessed the next opportunity.
Following up on a barrier
Context and barrier
An employee network could not see what happened after raising an issue.
Action taken
The coordinating team created an action log with a responsible role and review date.
What changed
Follow-up became visible; the barrier was not yet shown to be resolved.
Evidence and limits
An action log records activity, not proof that a barrier has been removed.
Lesson learned
Separate action taken from the result people experience.
Recommended next step
Have the responsible function report the result and seek network feedback at the next review.

About the network

About ARAN

The Anti-Racism Ambassadors Network (ARAN) is an independent, volunteer-led network of public servants across Canada. It supports efforts to dismantle systemic racism across all levels of government.

The network connects people working on anti-racism, shares practical resources and supports collective action. Its work centres the experiences of Black, Indigenous and racialized colleagues and considers how racism intersects with disability, gender, language and other barriers.

Public servants and allies can take part through learning, volunteering, sharing evidence or connecting with other employee networks.

Read the network overview on GCwiki

A volunteer coordinating team supports network communications, shared resources and participation. Contact the network mailbox for coordination and partnership requests.

Frequently asked questions
Can I join outside the federal public service?
Yes. ARAN connects public servants across municipal, provincial, territorial and federal government in Canada. Email the network to find the appropriate space.
Do I need manager approval?
You can browse public resources directly. Participation during work time depends on your organization’s arrangements. Discuss time, duties and any approval requirements with your manager.
How do I get the next meeting invitation?
Email the network for the date, time zone, meeting language, access link and accessibility arrangements. A public meeting date is not confirmed on this page.
Is participation confidential?
Public lessons are visible to anyone with the draft link. Network emails can be read by the volunteers managing the mailbox. Ask meeting organizers about privacy and recording before sharing personal experiences.
Is the site bilingual?
The website interface is available in English and French. Contributions stay in their original language. Resources, podcast audio and meeting language support vary; check the item or ask the organizers.
What happens after I send a lesson?
Direct submission adds your lesson immediately to the public working draft and gives you a reference and withdrawal code. Save the receipt. The draft provides a basis for future analysis; a submission is not a verified finding or network position.
Can I remove my lesson?
Yes. Open “Withdraw a contribution” in the contribution section and enter the reference and code on your receipt. This removes the active entry but cannot recall copies already downloaded or shared.
Are these official government tools?
ARAN and its independent tools are maintained by volunteers. Official references are labelled. Check the original published sources, definitions and dates before using data in a decision.

Privacy, retention and accessibility
Lessons sent through direct submission are public and remain in the active draft until withdrawn; there is no automatic expiry. Anyone with the link, including the coordinating team, can read them. Your receipt contains the reference and code required for removal. Downloaded or shared copies cannot be recalled. Email contributions and volunteer messages go to the network mailbox and are not published automatically. Ask the coordinating team about email retention or withdrawal.
Accessibility preferences and saved resource choices are stored on this device when storage is available. An unfinished lesson is saved only if you select that option. A language change temporarily transfers your form entries within this browser tab, then removes that transfer. This site uses no advertising or analytics cookies. Hosting and linked services may process technical connection data under their own policies.
Use the accessibility menu for text size, contrast, spacing, reading layout, link underlining and reduced motion. The site also supports keyboard navigation and visible focus. To report a barrier or request an alternative format, email the network with the page and task involved.
