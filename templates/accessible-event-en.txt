Before inviting participants
Confirm the purpose, intended audience and participation options.
Choose a venue or platform that supports the access needs identified.
Check routes, entrances, seating and washrooms for an in-person event.
Test keyboard access, audio, captions and shared materials for an online event.
Confirm the languages of the event and any interpretation or captioning arrangements.
Plan breaks and an alternative way to contribute.

Invitation and registration
Provide clear joining or travel instructions and the event’s start and end time.
Describe the accessibility arrangements that are actually available.
Give a contact and a private way to request an adjustment or another format.
Ask what support is needed to participate. Do not ask for a diagnosis.
Explain any registration information being collected and who will receive it.
State whether recording is planned and how it will be used.

Materials and speakers
Share accessible materials in advance when possible.
Use descriptive headings, readable text, sufficient contrast and meaningful link text.
Describe visual information that is needed to understand the discussion.
Provide text alternatives for relevant images and an alternative to scanned text.
Ask speakers to use a microphone, speak clearly and explain unfamiliar terms.
Confirm how participants can ask questions or contribute in writing.

During the event
Explain participation, captioning, language and access options at the start.
Test the microphone and caption display with participants.
Identify speakers and read relevant questions from chat aloud.
Keep to the planned pace and breaks. Allow people to leave and return.
Address access problems without asking participants to explain personal circumstances publicly.

After the event
Share accessible notes or materials through the agreed channel.
Review recordings or transcripts for accuracy before sharing them.
Offer a private way to give access feedback.
Record improvements for the next event without retaining unnecessary personal details.
