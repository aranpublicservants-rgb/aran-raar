Before you start

Some deadlines are short. An informal conversation, ombuds office or mediation process does not automatically pause a grievance or complaint deadline. Check your employer, agreement and the official filing rules immediately.

Start with three steps

You do not need to know legal language to begin. These steps help you identify the right place to turn.

  1. 1Describe the problem

    Record what happened, when you learned of it, who made the decision and what you want changed.

  2. 2Check who employs you

    Core federal departments, separate agencies, Crown corporations and special employment groups may have different rules.

  3. 3Check the time limit

    Find the official deadline before waiting for a meeting, an investigation or informal help.

Discrimination or a denied accommodation

You believe a work rule, decision or workplace barrier treats you unfairly because of a ground protected by the Canadian Human Rights Act.

Human rights complaint: generally within 12 months

What to do next
  1. Write down what happened, the dates, the barrier and what remedy you are seeking.
  2. Ask for accommodation or written reasons where relevant. An accommodation request does not have to be in writing.
  3. Check whether a grievance also applies. A grievance and a human rights complaint can have different deadlines.
  4. Contact the Canadian Human Rights Commission. It may consider whether another process should deal with the issue first.

Read the official source: Canadian Human Rights Commission: file a complaint

Workplace harassment or violence

You have experienced or witnessed conduct that may amount to harassment or violence at work, including behaviour linked to discrimination.

Current employees: no fixed deadline to give a notice of occurrence

What to do next
  1. Report the occurrence to your employer or designated recipient under the workplace policy.
  2. The employer or designated recipient must respond within 7 days and start a meeting for negotiated resolution within 45 days.
  3. You can ask about the investigation process and representation or support during resolution.
  4. Consider separate human rights or grievance options if discrimination, discipline or other employment issues are involved; those deadlines may differ.

Read the official source: Labour Program: file a notice of occurrence

An appointment or staffing process

You believe an appointment process was unfair or was affected by abuse of authority. The path depends on whether the process is internal or external.

Eligible internal staffing complaints: generally 15 calendar days after notice

What to do next
  1. Confirm whether the process is internal or external and whether the Public Service Employment Act applies.
  2. For an eligible internal complaint, check the Board’s notice and filing requirements immediately. Informal discussion does not replace filing.
  3. For an external appointment process, review the Public Service Commission’s investigation route instead.
  4. Keep the advertisement, screening results, notices, assessment records and correspondence.

Read the official source: Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board: staffing guide

Pay, leave or terms of employment

You believe your employer applied the wrong pay, leave, overtime or employment rule.

Individual grievance: often 35 calendar days under the federal regulations; check your agreement

What to do next
  1. Find the written decision, relevant pay record and applicable collective agreement or employment directive.
  2. Ask your bargaining agent or labour relations contact which grievance procedure applies.
  3. If the grievance interprets a collective agreement, the bargaining agent’s approval and representation are required.
  4. Present the grievance within the applicable period and keep the dated receipt. Verify each later step and any adjudication referral.

Read the official source: Federal Public Sector Labour Relations Regulations: section 68

Discipline, suspension, demotion or dismissal

Your employer has taken or proposed disciplinary or serious employment action against you.

Do not delay: an individual grievance may have a 35-day default, with other rules or exceptions

What to do next
  1. Get the letter, reasons and dates of notification. Keep a factual account of events.
  2. Contact a representative or obtain independent advice before meetings or filing, particularly if your position is excluded or unrepresented.
  3. Check which grievance rights and decision levels apply to your position.
  4. If a grievance is unsuccessful, verify whether your particular issue can be referred to the Board and the relevant referral deadline.

Read the official source: Labour Board: guide to individual grievances

Classification of your position

You disagree with the classification of your position, including the assigned group or level.

Core administration classification grievance: 35 calendar days after notification or awareness

What to do next
  1. Ask for your current work description, classification decision and evaluation rationale.
  2. Check the Treasury Board classification grievance process or the rules for your separate employer.
  3. Present the classification grievance through the designated process within the applicable period.
  4. Keep a record of the submission, receipt and any decision.

Read the official source: Treasury Board: classification grievances

Unsafe work or an immediate hazard

You have reasonable cause to believe that an activity, equipment or work location creates a danger under Part II of the Canada Labour Code.

Act immediately; the formal right-to-refuse process has specific steps and exceptions

What to do next
  1. If there is immediate danger, take appropriate immediate safety measures and notify your supervisor.
  2. State clearly if you are exercising the right to refuse dangerous work under section 128.
  3. Follow the employer and workplace health and safety committee investigation steps.
  4. If unresolved, consult the Labour Program’s official process. Not every unsafe condition qualifies for a work refusal.

Read the official source: Labour Program: right to refuse dangerous work

Wrongdoing disclosure and reprisal

You disclosed a serious wrongdoing or took part in a protected investigation and believe someone retaliated against you because of it.

Reprisal complaint to the Integrity Commissioner: 60 days from awareness; extension may be possible

What to do next
  1. Identify the protected disclosure or investigation and the action you believe was retaliation.
  2. Record when you first learned of the alleged reprisal and preserve supporting facts.
  3. Contact the Office of the Public Sector Integrity Commissioner promptly about its complaint procedure.
  4. A regular workplace dispute or retaliation for another kind of complaint may need a different process.

Read the official source: Public Sector Integrity Commissioner: reprisal complaints

Official-language rights

You believe an institution has not respected rights concerning English or French, including some language-of-work or position-language requirements.

Commissioner complaints: generally 6 months for Part IV or section 91; 12 months for other provisions

What to do next
  1. Identify the institution, incident, date and specific language-related concern.
  2. Check whether the right applies to your workplace, geographic region and type of situation.
  3. Contact the Office of the Commissioner of Official Languages; anonymous complaints have practical limits.
  4. If the matter also affects staffing or discrimination, check any separate and shorter deadlines.

Read the official source: Official Languages Commissioner: before filing

Find your bargaining agent or employment rules

You are unsure whether a union represents you or which grievance process applies.

Confirm your employment status before choosing a process

What to do next
  1. Check your letter of offer and your substantive position’s occupational group and employer.
  2. Use the Treasury Board collective-agreement index for core administration groups. Separate employers may have their own agreements.
  3. Ask your union or human resources contact to confirm representation and any special procedure.
  4. Do not assume a term employee is unrepresented. Casual workers, students, excluded positions and executives have different legal considerations.

Read the official source: Treasury Board: collective agreements and bargaining agents

Key filing time limits

These are reference points, not personalized legal deadlines. Dates may depend on your notice, employment status, agreement and governing law.

Key filing time limits
SituationGeneral rule to verify
Individual grievanceUsually 35 calendar days under section 68 of the federal labour relations regulations. A different applicable rule or agreement may change the result.
Referral of a grievance to adjudicationOften 40 calendar days after a final-level decision (or the prescribed decision period), unless a different rule applies.
Eligible internal staffing complaint15 calendar days after the relevant notice, with rules for weekends and holidays.
Classification grievance in the core administration35 calendar days after notice or knowledge, under Treasury Board guidance.
Discrimination complaint to the human rights commissionGenerally within 12 months; the Commission may consider exceptions.
Reprisal complaint under the disclosure protection law60 days after awareness; the Commissioner can extend in appropriate circumstances.
Harassment and violence notice by a current employeeNo fixed time limit for the notice of occurrence; other processes may have their own deadlines.
Official-language complaintGenerally 6 months for Part IV or section 91; 12 months for other provisions.

For individual grievances, federal regulations count calendar days unless a different provision applies. This website intentionally does not calculate filing dates: a wrong date could put your rights at risk.

Do you have a bargaining agent?

Being a federal public servant does not, by itself, identify your union or grievance rights.

  • Most employees in a bargaining unit can seek help from their union. A grievance about interpreting or applying a collective agreement generally requires bargaining-agent approval and representation.
  • An employee on a term appointment may still be represented. Casual, student, excluded, unrepresented and executive positions must be considered separately.
  • A separate agency or Crown corporation may have different agreements and legislation. Check your employer and appointment letter before relying on a deadline.

Find your collective agreement and bargaining agent

An important distinction

An individual grievance, a staffing complaint, a discrimination complaint and a harassment notice are different processes. More than one may apply, but their conditions and deadlines differ.

Ombuds offices and informal conflict management services may help, but they do not adjudicate legal disputes or automatically stop filing deadlines.
